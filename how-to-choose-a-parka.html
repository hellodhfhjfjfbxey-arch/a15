How to Choose a Parka

A good parka keeps you warm on the coldest, windiest days. Five decisions make the difference between a coat you love and one that leaves you shivering.

-8°Cstart temperature

Day 01-8°CClear

Match warmth to your winter

Think about the coldest weather you actually face, not the average day. A city parka for temperatures around freezing needs far less insulation than one for prairie or northern winters. Manufacturers often give a comfort rating; treat it as a guide, since your activity and layers matter just as much.

Day 02-12°CWind

Choose a length

Length decides how much of you stays warm.

  • Hip length is easiest to move in and suits active use.
  • Thigh length covers the seat and suits most cold cities.
  • Knee length is warmest when standing still or waiting outside.
Day 03-16°CSnow

Look closely at the hood

A deep, adjustable hood with a stiffened brim protects your face from wind. A ruff of real or faux fur around the edge creates a pocket of calmer, warmer air in front of your face. Check the hood turns with your head and fits over a hat.

Day 04-20°CWhiteout

Shell and details

The outer fabric should block wind and shrug off snow. In wet climates choose a waterproof, taped shell. Look for a two-way zip with a storm flap, inner cuffs, fleece-lined hand-warmer pockets and a drawcord at the waist or hem to trap warmth.

Day 05-24°CClear

Get the fit right

Try a parka on over the thickest layer you will wear underneath. You should be able to cross your arms and raise them without the hem lifting or the cuffs pulling up. Too tight compresses the insulation and makes it less warm; too loose lets cold air in.
