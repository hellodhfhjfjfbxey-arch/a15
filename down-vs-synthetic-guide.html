Down vs Synthetic

The insulation inside a parka decides how warm it is, how much it weighs and how it copes with wet weather. Here is how down and synthetic fill compare.

-14°Cstart temperature

Day 01-14°CClear

How insulation works

Neither down nor synthetic fill makes heat. Both trap still air warmed by your body, and still air is a very good insulator. The more air a fill can trap for its weight, and the better it springs back after being squashed, the warmer and lighter the coat.

Day 02-18°CWind

Down and fill power

Down is the soft under-plumage of ducks and geese. Fill power measures how much space one ounce of down fills.

  • 550 to 650: good everyday warmth.
  • 700 to 800: very warm for its weight.
  • Over 800: premium, for the lightest, warmest coats.

Look for responsibly sourced down certifications.

Day 03-22°CSnow

Synthetic fill

Synthetic insulation is made of fine polyester fibres. It is a little heavier and bulkier than down for the same warmth, but it keeps much of its warmth when damp, dries quickly and is usually cheaper and easier to wash.

Day 04-26°CWhiteout

Wet weather

Down clumps and loses its loft when soaked, so it is best in dry cold or under a waterproof shell. In wet snow, sleet and coastal winters, synthetic fill is the safer choice. Some parkas use treated, water-resistant down or put synthetic fill in the shoulders and hood.

Day 05-30°CClear

Care and storage

Wash insulated coats rarely, with a cleaner made for down or synthetics, then tumble dry on low with clean tennis balls to restore loft. Store parkas hanging or loosely folded, never compressed in a stuff sack, so the insulation keeps its spring.
